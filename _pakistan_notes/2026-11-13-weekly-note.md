**The week in one line:** remittances rose 13.8% to $3.89bn in October and the 12-month T-bill yield fell to 12.41%, but Brent climbed back to $95.82 a barrel.

## What changed this week

- **Remittances kept rising.** Workers abroad sent $3.89bn in October, 13.8% more than a year earlier, [the State Bank reported](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40448227/pakistan-records-39bn-in-remittances-for-october-2026), taking July–October to $14.77bn, up 14%. Saudi Arabia and the UAE were again the largest sources.
- **Borrowing got cheaper.** At the 11 Nov T-bill auction the government [raised Rs587bn against a Rs400bn target](https://www.dawn.com/news/2043893/govt-raises-rs587bn-via-t-bill-auction), with bids of Rs2.19tr. The 12-month cut-off [fell 48 bp to 12.41%](https://www.investify.pk/news/100000161177/govt-raises-rs550-billion-in-t-bill-auction-as-yields-drop-by-up-to-48-basis-points), from 12.89% two weeks earlier.
- **The trade gap widened.** The goods trade deficit reached $14.63bn in July–October, [according to PBS data](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40447880/4mfy27-trade-deficit-widens-1597pc-yoy), with imports of $25.96bn more than twice exports of $11.34bn.
- **Oil turned up again.** Brent [settled at $95.82 a barrel](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/world/40073442) on 12 Nov, up $3.06 on the day, after reported attacks on Saudi energy sites and tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. The government [trimmed petrol and diesel prices](https://www.radio.gov.pk/12-11-2026/govt-revises-petroleum-prices-for-today) again this week, before the rebound.
- **Stocks slipped.** The KSE-100 [fell 0.95% on 12 Nov](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40448547/psx-selling-conintues-kse-100-down-nearly-1) to close at 177,374, still 1.9% up on the year. The rupee closed at 276.61 per dollar.
- **Reserves barely moved.** The SBP's own reserves [rose $11m in the week to 6 Nov](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40448606/sbp-held-foreign-exchange-reserves-rise-11-million-to-2157-billion), to $21.57bn; total liquid reserves were $26.84bn.
- **The IMF Board is now expected in December.** The Executive Board is [likely to take up the fourth review next month](https://www.nation.com.pk/08-Nov-2026/imf-consider-approving-dollar-1-2b-disbursement-pakistan-next-month), which would release about $1.2bn.

## Where things stand

| | Latest | For comparison |
|---|---|---|
| CPI inflation | 9.6% (Oct) | 10.3% in Sep |
| Policy rate | 12.00% | +2.4 pp above headline inflation |
| 12-month T-bill | 12.41% (11 Nov) | 12.89% on 28 Oct |
| SBP reserves | $21.57bn (6 Nov) | goal: $23bn by end-Dec |
| Remittances, Jul–Oct | $14.77bn | +14% y/y |
| Goods trade deficit, Jul–Oct | $14.63bn | exports $11.34bn |

**Workers abroad are holding up the external accounts.** Remittances of $14.77bn in the first four months of the year are roughly the size of the $14.63bn goods trade deficit. They have kept the current-account deficit, −$874m in July–September, small even as imports have grown. October's current account, due in the coming days, will show whether the higher oil bill has tipped it further into deficit.

**Markets see less need for another rate rise.** The fall in bill yields after the October hike suggests banks expect the policy rate to stay at 12.00% for now. Overnight money traded at 12.34% on 11 Nov, inside the State Bank's corridor. Private-sector credit grew 16.8% in the year to 30 Oct, so firms are still borrowing despite higher rates.

**Inflation is not beaten.** Headline inflation was 9.6% in October, but the weekly basket of essentials was 12.31% dearer than a year earlier in the week to 5 Nov, and oil is rising again. Reserves, at $21.57bn, are still $1.43bn short of the end-December goal.

## Coming up

- **Mid-November:** October's current account from the State Bank.
- **December, second week (expected):** IMF Executive Board on the fourth review.
- **Early December:** November CPI from the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics.
- **14 Dec:** the State Bank's next rate decision.

## What to watch

Whether oil holds near this week's higher level; whether October's current account shows the higher import bill; and whether SBP reserves pick up pace towards the $23bn goal.
