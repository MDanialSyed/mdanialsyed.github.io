**The week in one line:** the current account showed a $39m surplus in October and private-sector credit grew 17.4% in the year to 6 Nov, but the weekly price basket was still 11.21% dearer than a year earlier.

## What changed this week

- **The current account turned positive.** October showed a $39m surplus, [the State Bank reported](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40449346/pakistan-posts-39mn-current-account-surplus-in-oct-2026), the first of FY27, after a September deficit now put at −$371m. That cut the July–October deficit to −$850m, from −$1,062m a year earlier. Analysts [credited remittances and exports](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40449481/ca-posts-first-surplus-of-fy27-since-may-2026).
- **Firms borrowed more.** Private-sector credit grew 17.4% in the year to 6 Nov, up from 16.8% a week earlier, according to [State Bank data](https://easydata.sbp.org.pk/). Broad money growth slowed to 11.4%, after the State Bank revised the 30 Oct figure down to 12.4%.
- **Car sales jumped.** Passenger car sales reached 23,432 in October, [41% more than a year earlier](https://www.pakwheels.com/blog/pakistan-car-sales-jump-63-in-october-2026/) and up from 14,406 in September.
- **Fuel cuts eased the weekly basket.** The SPI [fell 0.46% in the week to 12 Nov](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40448848/weekly-spi-inflation-down-046pc) as petrol and diesel got cheaper, but it was still 11.21% above a year earlier, with tomatoes, eggs and flour dearer on the week.
- **The State Bank looked back on a hard year.** Its [annual report for FY26](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2640887/despite-shocks-economic-resilience-strengthens), out on 17 Nov, said the economy had absorbed this year's shocks, with reserves above their end-June target, while noting inflation's return to double digits from April.
- **Stocks kept slipping.** The KSE-100 [fell 0.69% on 18 Nov](https://arynews.tv/psx-stock-ends-lower-as-kse-100-index-falls-over-1000) to close at 176,294, leaving it 1.3% up on the year. The rupee was at 276.55 per dollar.
- **Oil steadied.** Brent [settled at $93.14 a barrel](https://financefeeds.com/brent-crude-oil-price-92-61-wednesday-us-stock-build-fed-minutes-bull-96-bear-88/) on 17 Nov, up $0.70, as recovering Gulf exports offset new attacks on shipping near Hormuz.

## Where things stand

| | Latest | For comparison |
|---|---|---|
| CPI inflation | 9.6% (Oct) | 10.3% in Sep |
| Policy rate | 12.00% | +2.4 pp above headline inflation |
| Current account, Jul–Oct | −$850m | −$1,062m a year earlier |
| Private-sector credit | +17.4% (6 Nov) | +16.8% on 30 Oct |
| SBP reserves | $21.57bn (6 Nov) | goal: $23bn by end-Dec |
| Weekly price basket (SPI) | 11.21% y/y (12 Nov) | −0.46% on the week |

**The external accounts are holding, for now.** Remittances of $3.89bn in October were enough to tip the current account into a $39m surplus, even with the goods trade deficit at $14.63bn in July–October. One month does not make a trend: oil at $93.14 a barrel still weighs on the import bill.

**Credit is growing despite higher rates.** Private-sector credit at 17.4% and car sales of 23,432 point to demand picking up, even with the policy rate at 12.00% and overnight money at 12.48%. Factory output, up 1.85% in July–August, has yet to show the same pace.

**Households are still squeezed.** Headline inflation was 9.6% in October, and the weekly basket of essentials was 11.21% dearer than a year earlier. Reserves, at $21.57bn, are $1.43bn short of the end-December goal, so the State Bank has little room to ease.

## Coming up

- **Thursdays:** SBP weekly reserves, the first test of whether they pick up pace towards the $23bn goal.
- **Early December:** November CPI from the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics.
- **December (expected):** IMF Executive Board on the fourth review.
- **14 Dec:** the State Bank's next rate decision.

## What to watch

Whether November's CPI confirms that inflation is easing; whether reserves start to climb towards the end-December goal; and whether the IMF Board sets a date for the fourth review.
