**The week in one line:** factory output grew 2.45% in September from a year earlier, lifting July–September growth to 2.07%, while SBP reserves rose just $15m to $21.59bn.

## What changed this week

- **Factories picked up.** Large-scale manufacturing grew 2.45% in September from a year earlier and 3.18% from August, [the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics reported](https://www.pbs.gov.pk/summary-of-provisional-quantum-index-numbers-of-large-scale-manufacturing-industries-qim-for-august-2026/) on 19 Nov. That took growth for July–September to 2.07%. Garments, cars and petroleum products [did most of the lifting](https://nukta.com/pakistan-large-scale-manufacturing-september), while food, chemicals, textiles and medicines held output back.
- **Reserves barely moved.** SBP reserves rose $15m to $21.59bn in the week to 13 Nov, [the State Bank said](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40449754/sbp-held-foreign-exchange-reserves-rise-15-million-to-2159-billion). Total liquid reserves, including the banks' $5.19bn, were $26.77bn.
- **Money-market rates firmed.** The overnight repo rate rose to 12.55% on 18 Nov, from 12.48% a day earlier, according to [State Bank data](https://easydata.sbp.org.pk/), just above the 12.00% policy rate.
- **Stocks bounced.** The KSE-100 [rose 0.27% on 19 Nov](https://arynews.tv/psx-stock-closes-higher-as-kse-100-index-gains-over-400-points) to close at 176,772, leaving it 1.6% up on the year. The rupee was at 276.55 per dollar on 18 Nov.
- **Oil steadied.** Brent [settled at $91.98 a barrel](https://www.rigzone.com/news/wire/oil_prices_swing_on_middle_east_uncertainty-19-nov-2026-185129-article/) on 19 Nov, as worries about renewed tension with Iran offset improving Gulf supplies.

## Where things stand

| | Latest | For comparison |
|---|---|---|
| CPI inflation | 9.6% (Oct) | 10.3% in Sep |
| Policy rate | 12.00% | +2.4 pp above headline inflation |
| Large-scale manufacturing, Jul–Sep | +2.07% | +2.45% in Sep alone |
| Private-sector credit | +17.4% (6 Nov) | broad money +11.4% |
| SBP reserves | $21.59bn (13 Nov) | goal: $23bn by end-Dec |
| Current account, Jul–Oct | −$850m | −$1,062m a year earlier |

**Industry is catching up with credit.** September's 2.45% rise in factory output is the first sign that faster borrowing, with private-sector credit up 17.4%, and stronger car sales, at 23,432 in October, are reaching production. Cement despatches, up just 1.2% in October, show the recovery is still uneven.

**The external cushion is growing slowly.** Reserves added $15m on the week and remain $1.41bn short of the end-December goal. Remittances of $3.89bn in October tipped the current account into a $39m surplus, but more industrial activity will mean more imports, and oil at $91.98 a barrel keeps the bill high.

**Prices limit the State Bank.** Headline inflation was 9.6% in October and the weekly basket of essentials was still 11.21% dearer than a year earlier in the week to 12 Nov. With the policy rate at 12.00%, there is little room to ease.

## Coming up

- **Thursdays:** SBP weekly reserves, still well short of the pace needed for the $23bn goal.
- **Early December:** November CPI from the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics.
- **December (expected):** IMF Executive Board on the fourth review.
- **14 Dec:** the State Bank's next rate decision.

## What to watch

Whether October's factory data keep September's pace; whether November's CPI confirms that inflation is easing; and whether reserves start to climb towards the end-December goal.
